## About section (website, ~230 words)

I learned to code at 14, training for informatics olympiads. Years of contests taught me one habit that matters more than any framework: break a messy problem into small parts, solve each one correctly, and test it before you trust it. That habit earned medals at the Republic and Eurasian Olympiads and a diploma at the ICPC Northern Eurasia Finals.

Code alone doesn't help a business, so I learned the other half. At SimpleGov 2023, a design sprint run by UsabilityLab and Kazakhstan's Ministry of Digital Development, my team interviewed real users of eGov.kz, mapped their pain points, tested prototypes and won. We presented to the Minister. I later completed a product management program and built a Chrome extension at nFactorial Incubator, one of 180 people selected from 4,000+ applicants.

For two years I mentored at Miras Campus, a student residence of 150 to 200 students. I started with a group of 10 first-year students and ended up leading a team of mentors. We ran weekly events, skill workshops and guest lectures, and helped students drop bad habits and build routines that held.

That's how I approach automation. First I learn how your team works. Then I find the hours that leak into repetitive tasks and build AI workflows that are reliable enough to forget about. When I hand them over, I explain them so your people trust them and keep using them.

**I hand over, I don't lock in.** Coaching taught me to explain until it clicks. You get documentation and a team that understands what runs in the background.
